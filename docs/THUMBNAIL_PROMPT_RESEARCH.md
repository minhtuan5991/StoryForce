## Applied direction

- A short curiosity headline complements the canonical video title and promises only what the story delivers. It withholds the answer rather than inventing a shock, true-story label or unrelated monster.
- The user's explicit preference is 2–3 typefaces: bold condensed keywords, a different thinner but readable supporting face, and an optional restrained third face. These font choices are a requested design direction, not a claim that every reference channel uses them.
- Exactly two contrasting text/accent colors must remain legible against the background. Warm amber and icy white on blue/slate, or light text with a bright red accent, are examples rather than mandatory palettes for every genre.
- Mystery comes from a concrete clue, framing and controlled shadows. A single scene and one text group should stay readable at 320 × 180, with safe margins and the bottom-right duration-badge area clear. Other channel genres retain their own emotional tone.
- Scene images use practical lighting, natural colors, credible faces/materials/perspective and established continuity. The scene's actual time of day and weather take priority. An impossible detail is allowed only when the story specifies it; the surrounding world remains believable.
